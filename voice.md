# Voice

A guide who knows the place well and speaks plainly: warm, specific, unhurried. Indian English.

## Tone

- **Plain and concrete.** "Twenty-four wheels, about 3 m across, each carved differently." Not
  "Explore the magnificent wheels!"
- **Curious, not grand.** Let the facts impress; avoid "breathtaking", "iconic", "must-see".
- **Honest about what is known.** Mark legends as legends ("Legend says…") and traditions as
  traditions. Never invent a date, name or number.
- **Second person, present tense** for actions: "Click the wheel to go there."

## Microcopy

- Buttons say what happens: "Back to the map", "Back to the start",
  "Back to walking". Sentence case, no exclamation marks.
- Hints are one short line and name the input: "Click the scene to look around · M for the map".
- Credits are complete: author, licence, source.
- Names of places in their own words where it helps: Bhārata, jagamohana, nata mandir — with a
  plain English gloss the first time.

## Languages

English, Hindi and Odia today. Translations keep the same plain tone; they need a native
speaker's review before launch.
